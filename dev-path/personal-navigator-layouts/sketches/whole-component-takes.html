Three whole-component takes

The Salesforce Navigator, drawn three ways in the Rootstock UI kit's language. Each take varies structure, density and control placement together, so the choice is one decision rather than three. Not a build: no LWC, no SLDS classes, no base components.

The question. The spec's ## Open questions closed at the design gate, and the built component styles itself from SLDS 2 hooks with no visual language of its own. Which whole-component treatment does the Navigator adopt — and what does that cost against the Outcomes that are already built?
The dark switch flips color-scheme and touches nothing else — every value is a real light-dark(), as Cosmos authors them.
Take A

Nested cards

What is built today, re-tuned to the kit: a section stays a card, but drops to a 12px radius and a hairline inside the 20px shell, and the item loses its heavy border to become a quiet row that colours blue on hover. The section reads unmistakably as a draggable object.

First open, seeded — the density that actually ships
A bare org returned 174 nav items. This panel renders that count for real, at this take's density, in the one seeded "All Items" section the spec computes and does not persist.
What it buys
  • A section looks grabbable, which matters — section reorder is a drag, and the card is the drag source.
  • Closest to what exists: navigatorSection.css changes values, not structure.
  • The nested-card rule is in the kit already — 20px shell, smaller radius inside.
What it costs
  • Three nested boxes at the seeded 174 items: shell, card, row. The heaviest of the three.
  • The kit's hairline border contradicts the built CSS comment that "SLDS 2 removes borders from cards". One of the two has to give.
  • Vertical rhythm eats space a 6-column section wants.
What changes
  • navigatorSection.css — radius border-2 → border-3, shadow out, hairline in.
  • navigatorItem.css — drop border-2, add hover colour.
  • No template changes in any bundle.
Take B

Flat bands

One card, not many. A section becomes the kit's inset band — a #F3F3F3 strip carrying the name as a micro-label plus its controls — and its items sit on the white surface beneath it. This is the take that most reads as a better All Items list, which is what the Intent says the component is.

What it buys
  • Lightest of the three at 174 items — one box, not three.
  • The band is a kit primitive used exactly as the kit uses it: labels, headers, totals.

What it costs
  • The real one. A section stops looking like an object you can pick up, and section reorder is drag-based. The drag target becomes the band, and that has to be taught.

What changes
  • navigatorSection.html — the <article> keeps draggable, but the visible grab affordance moves to the band.
  • navigatorSection.css — card styles out, band styles in; both drag states re-drawn.
  • Re-check the grabbed and drop-target signals against Outcome 6.
Take C

Pill grid

Items become the kit's pills and flow rather than sit in tracks. Densest by a wide margin, and the most obviously "a set of things you chose". The item menu is not drawn until the pill is hovered or focused — which is the whole argument against it.

  • By far the densest at 174 items, and the least box-in-box.
  • Label length stops mattering — no track to overflow, no text-overflow.

What it costs
  • Two Outcomes take damage. A flowing wrap has no columns, so "set its column count between one and six" has nothing to set. And a hover-revealed menu is the cross-section move mechanism for keyboard users — it must still be reachable by Tab, so the reveal has to be :focus-within, never hover alone.

What changes
  • navigatorSection — the cols-1…6 grid classes and the column menu come out. That is Outcome 4 and slice 03.
  • navigatorItem.css — full rewrite.
  • Re-verify the arrow-key move pattern: "next item" in a wrap is not "next item" in a grid.

Three things worth knowing before you pick

  • Mapping the kit by value is a trap, and it is the exact trap the spec names. Ask the metadata which hook holds #181818 and it answers --slds-g-color-palette-neutral-10 and --slds-g-color-neutral-base-10 — both in the family of 38 the spec flags as having no light-dark(). They pass a "uses a hook" check and then sit fixed and unreadable in dark mode. Every row above is mapped by role instead, which is why every one of them flips.
  • A contrast failure this sketch caught by being switchable, and would not have caught otherwise — with a rule that outlives whichever take you pick. The first draft filled the primary button with --slds-g-color-accent-2, the kit's own blue, and set its label to --slds-g-color-on-accent-1. Light mode: white on #0250d9, 6.66:1, fine. Flip to dark and accent-2 becomes #7cb1fe while on-accent-1 stays a fixed #fff — 2.19:1. Pairing by index instead (on-accent-2, which flips) only reaches 3.04:1: enough for large text, and a 14px bold button label is not large text.

    The rule, measured rather than assumed. Accent has two jobs and they take different hooks. As a fill under a label — buttons, selected chips — use accent-1 + on-accent-1: both are fixed, so they cannot drift apart, and the pair holds 4.67:1 in both modes. That is what every filled control on this page now uses. The cost is honest: #066afe is a step brighter than the kit's #0050D9, and that step is what buys the contrast. As ink or line — link text, hover colour, focus ring, selected border — use accent-2, which is the kit's blue exactly and reads 6.66:1 light / 7.08:1 dark against the card.
  • One inaccuracy in the built CSS, found on the way here. navigatorSection.css has color: var(--slds-g-color-on-surface-3, #181818) with the comment that this is the title colour. The hook is right; the fallback is not — under Cosmos it resolves to #03234d. The fallback only shows if the hook is ever missing, so nothing is visibly wrong today, but it means the file records the wrong intent.
